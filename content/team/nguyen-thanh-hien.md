I have always been drawn to the idea that every organisation — no matter how large or small — runs on a set of decisions, and that better information leads to better decisions. This conviction is what drew me to business analysis: the discipline of finding clarity in complexity.

At HSB University, I concentrate my studies on understanding how organisations function at a process level, and where the gap between what is planned and what actually happens creates inefficiency or opportunity. A group project in my second year gave me hands-on experience mapping operational workflows for a student-run enterprise. Working through that project, I realised how often friction lives in undocumented hand-offs — places where one person assumes the next person already has the information they need.

Beyond coursework, I am developing my skills in data visualisation and structured reporting. I believe that the hardest part of analysis is not finding the answer but communicating it in a way that makes action obvious. A beautiful chart that nobody acts on is still a failure.

I am methodical by nature, but I try not to let precision become slowness. In a team context, I aim to be the person who keeps the group anchored in facts while the creative work happens around me.
