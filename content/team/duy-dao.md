I have always been drawn to the question of why people choose one brand over another — not just the functional rationale, but the emotional logic underneath it. Growing up in Ho Chi Minh City, a place where Western brand culture and deeply local identity coexist in fascinating tension, gave me an early curiosity about how meaning gets attached to products. That curiosity became the foundation of everything I study today.

As a third-year Business Administration student, I have concentrated my coursework on consumer behaviour and strategic communications. My most significant project to date was a large-scale consumer insights study into Gen-Z beverage preferences across three city districts. We interviewed 48 participants and surveyed a further 160, ultimately identifying three emergent loyalty triggers that the existing marketing literature had not yet named. The report was presented to a guest panel from an FMCG company, who incorporated two of our segmentation recommendations into their next seasonal campaign brief.

Beyond research, I am equally drawn to the craft of execution. During my second year, I collaborated with a local streetwear brand on a six-week campus activation. We had a minimal budget, so every creative decision had to earn its place — the resulting campaign grew their student-segment social following by 34% and was featured in the university's business faculty newsletter as a case study in lean marketing.

Outside of my studies, I run a small design side project creating brand identity packages for student-run businesses in our faculty. It keeps my visual skills sharp and constantly reminds me that strategy without aesthetics is just a spreadsheet with opinions.
