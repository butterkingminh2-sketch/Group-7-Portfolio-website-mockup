Marketing, at its core, is the discipline of understanding people — what they want before they can articulate it, what problems they feel before they can name them. This is the question I find myself returning to whenever I start a new project: what is the person on the other side of this message actually experiencing?

Growing up, I watched businesses in my neighbourhood succeed or struggle based not on the quality of their product alone but on whether they could communicate clearly and memorably. That observation planted an early interest in how language and image combine to move people to action — an interest I have since formalised through my studies in business administration.

At HSB University, I focus my coursework on marketing strategy and consumer psychology. I have developed a particular interest in how brands operating in saturated categories differentiate themselves through voice and positioning rather than product innovation alone. My group project analysing digital campaign performance across social platforms gave me practical experience in reading metrics as human signals rather than just numbers.

Outside of coursework, I enjoy exploring design fundamentals and applying them to personal projects. I believe that understanding visual communication makes me a more complete strategist — someone who can evaluate not just what is said but how it is shown.
