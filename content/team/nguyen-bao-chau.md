Numbers tell stories — you just have to know how to read them. This is the belief that has shaped my approach to studying business finance. Where some see spreadsheets as tedious, I find them clarifying. A well-constructed financial model is one of the cleanest ways to test whether an idea actually holds together.

At HSB University, I focus on the quantitative side of business administration, particularly financial analysis and structured research methodology. A project I am most proud of involved auditing the financial health of several student-run campus enterprises. It was a humbling exercise in how to ask the right questions — we went in expecting to find one pattern and came out with something far more nuanced.

I am drawn to research because it forces intellectual honesty. You cannot argue with well-collected data, even when it contradicts your hypothesis. That discipline carries over into how I approach group projects: I prefer to anchor discussions in evidence before moving to recommendations.

Outside of my studies, I am developing my skills in statistical analysis and working toward a stronger foundation in financial modelling. I believe that the ability to move fluidly between raw data and meaningful narrative is one of the most valuable skills a business student can build.
