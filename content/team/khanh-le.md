Business development, at its core, is about seeing value before others do — in a market, in a relationship, in a gap between what exists and what is needed. That instinct for potential is something I have been developing since secondary school, when I helped a family friend map the expansion of their printing business into two new districts. The project was informal, the analysis was rough, but the experience of sitting across from someone and helping them see their own opportunity more clearly has never left me.

As a third-year Business Administration student, I have concentrated on growth strategy and partnership development, with a particular interest in the Vietnamese startup ecosystem. My largest project to date was a comprehensive ecosystem report covering 80 Vietnamese tech startups across five verticals. Commissioned informally by a local angel investor network, the report mapped funding stages, investor activity, and founder backgrounds, and concluded with a sector-level opportunity matrix the network used to structure their next round of deal-scouting activities. The 60-page document took four months to produce and taught me more about research discipline than any single course I have taken.

My second major project applied business development logic to a real commercial opportunity. I developed a full go-to-market partnership proposal for an EdTech platform seeking to enter the Vietnamese university market, identifying 12 priority institutional targets through a combination of desk research and informal stakeholder interviews, and modelling revenue potential under three partnership structures. The proposal was presented to the platform's founding team, who adapted sections of it for their actual outreach materials.

I am at my best in rooms where the problem is not yet fully defined — where the goal is to identify the question before answering it. That is where I find business development most satisfying: not in the close, but in the clarity that makes the close possible.
