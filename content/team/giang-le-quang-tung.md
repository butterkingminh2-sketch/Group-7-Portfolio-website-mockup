Strategy is the art of knowing which problems to solve and in which order. This deceptively simple idea is what makes business fascinating to me — the fact that the same set of resources, organised differently, can produce entirely different outcomes.

At HSB University, I gravitate toward courses that sit at the boundary between analysis and decision-making. I am particularly interested in how organisations identify and respond to emerging opportunity before their competitors recognise it as such. A group project in strategic planning gave me the chance to build a full market entry framework from first principles, including stakeholder mapping, competitive positioning, and a phased go-to-market timeline. That experience reinforced my belief that rigour and creativity are not opposites — the best strategies need both.

I approach problems by first expanding the solution space as wide as possible, then narrowing deliberately. I find that most strategic errors come from committing to a direction before all the viable alternatives have been considered.

Outside of academics, I am interested in how design thinking methods can be adapted for business contexts — using tools like journey mapping and assumption testing to stress-test ideas before committing significant resources to them.
